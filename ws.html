Agnosias & Neglect

Answer Key

1. What: temporal lobe; Where: parietal lobe.

2. Damage to motion area (V5) in the Where pathway → inability to perceive motion.

3. Color blindness = retinal damage; Achromatopsia = lesion in cortical color area.

4. Yes – face recognition uses a separate module.

5. No – they retain the memory and can use other senses (e.g., touch).

6. Right hemisphere attends to both sides; left attends only to the right. A right‐hemisphere lesion causes left‐side neglect.

7. As Left‐side awareness returns, simultaneous stimuli are still biased toward the Right.

8. V1 damage = no left‐side input; neglect = input arrives but isn’t consciously processed.

9. Spatial regions of the Where (dorsal) pathway.

10. No – object, word, face, landmark recognition use different modules in the temporal lobe.

Dementia

Answer Key

1. Alzheimer’s disease.

2. Alzheimer’s onset ~70–80 yrs; FTD onset ~50–60 yrs.

3. New memory formation difficulty.

4. Anomic/empty speech, circumlocution, sound & word substitutions, comprehension + production deficits.

5. Tau neurofibrillary tangles & amyloid plaques.

6. Alzheimer’s: temporal & parietal; FTD: frontal & temporal.

7. Thinned gyri, widened sulci, enlarged ventricles.

8. ↑acetylcholine drugs & ↓β‑amyloid drugs; symptomatic relief only.

9. Disinhibition, compulsivity, poor judgment, socially inappropriate behavior.

10. Severe depression; B12/folate deficiency; hypothyroidism; certain infections (e.g., neurosyphilis).

Language Disorders

Answer Key

1. Semantic = wrong word; Phonemic = wrong sound.

2. Fluency, naming, repetition, comprehension, reading, writing, praxis.

3. Left hemisphere (∼99% right‑handers; ∼70% left‑handers).

4. Nonfluent speech with good comprehension.

5. Arcuate fasciculus.

6. Superior temporal gyrus.

7. Automatic speech, singing, simple sentence comprehension.

8. Visual aids, drawing, written responses, mouth‑movement training, melodic intonation therapy.

9. Not always; reading/writing often co‑impacted but can be relatively spared.

10. Yes in natural settings; cannot follow verbal commands.

Stroke & TBI

Answer Key

1. Lateral surface of the brain.

2. Middle (MCA) & anterior cerebral arteries (ACA).

3. Left MCA.

4. Echocardiogram.

5. Plaque buildup narrows arteries, → thrombosis or occlusion → ischemic stroke.

6. Lacunar stroke: small‐vessel infarcts in deep structures; can be as severe as large‐vessel strokes.

7. Carotid ultrasound/MR angiography, echo, EKG, blood tests; start anticoagulants or endarterectomy; risk‐factor control.

8. Ruptured artery bleeding into the subarachnoid space outside the brain.

9. Hypertension, AVM, cerebral aneurysm.

10. Bright (high‐density) area on CT.

11. Diffuse axonal injury.

12. Inferior frontal and anterior temporal lobes.

13. Subdural = venous bleed (lower pressure) vs epidural = arterial.

14. Hematoma & contusion.

15. Diffusion tensor imaging (DTI); reduced tract volume in injury vs healthy.
